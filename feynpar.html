Integration by parts identities in Feynman parametrisation

This project started as my first approach to research. My master's advisor at the time, Lorenzo Magnea , told me that, while preparing a lecture in honour of the late Tullio Regge , he had come across some interesting old articles related to a problem that is still relevant today.

When computing scattering amplitudes in weakly coupled theories, perturbation theory allows us to express S-matrix elements in terms of integrals, commonly known as Feynman integrals. These integrals form a vector space: integration-by-parts identities provide relations among them that can be used to reduce any integral to a linear combination of basis elements. Integral reduction, however, is a notoriously difficult problem and remains one of the major bottlenecks in the computation of scattering amplitudes.

Together with Lorenzo, we decided to explore what happens to integration-by-parts identities and to the reduction problem when the integrals are written in a different parametrisation: Feynman parameters instead of momentum space. The resulting reduction algorithm is still far from being as optimised as more established methods, but it offers fascinating insights into the algebraic structure of Feynman integrals. We continue to work on incorporating modern algebraic tools, such as Gröbner bases, into Feynman-integral reduction.
